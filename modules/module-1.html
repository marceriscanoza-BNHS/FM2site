Concept 1: Random Experiment

A repeatable process where you know all possible results in advance, but cannot predict the exact outcome of any single trial with certainty.

The 3 Pillars of a Random Experiment

  1. Repeatable under identical conditions on the workshop floor.
  2. All possible outcomes are known in advance.
  3. The specific result depends entirely on chance.

Master Inventory

Concept 2: Sample Space (S) & Cardinality

The Sample Space (S) is the universal container representing the master inventory of every single possible outcome. Nothing can be left out.

Set Notation: S = {ω₁, ω₂, ω₃, ...}

Sample Point (ω): An individual outcome in the sample space.

Cardinality (n(S)): The total count of all possible unique outcomes in S.

The Cardinality Shortcut Rules

1. Identical Binary/Multi-Option Stages (n(S) = kⁿ):

Where k = options available per stage (e.g., 2 for Pass/Fail) and n = number of stages (e.g., 3 inspected garments).

Example: n(S) = 2³ = 8 outcomes.

2. Multi-Variable Independent Choices:

Multiply choices across stages: n(S) = n₁ × n₂ × n₃ ...

Example: 3 Fabric Choices × 2 Sleeve Designs × 4 Embroidery Patterns = 24 total unique dress configurations.

Event Classification

Concept 3: Events (E) – Simple vs. Compound

An Event (E) is a specific collection of outcomes that satisfy a particular quality condition. Mathematically, an event is a subset of the master sample space: E ⊆ S.

Simple Event

Cardinality n(E) = 1

Contains exactly ONE specific element from S.

Example: Target E₁: "All three garments pass inspection."
E₁ = {(P, P, P)}, n(E₁) = 1.

Compound Event

Cardinality n(E) > 1

Contains TWO OR MORE elements from S.

Example: Target E₂: "At least two garments fail inspection."
E₂ = {(P,F,F), (F,P,F), (F,F,P), (F,F,F)}, n(E₂) = 4.

Troubleshooting

3 Critical Quality Control Misconceptions

1. The Sequential Trap: (P, F) ≠ (F, P)

The Error: Assuming "1 Pass and 1 Fail" is a single combined outcome, leading to S = {2 Pass, 1 Pass 1 Fail, 2 Fail} with incorrect n(S) = 3.

The Correction: Chronological order dictates physical identity on the factory floor. Shirt A Passing then Shirt B Failing is a completely different physical event than Shirt A Failing then Shirt B Passing.

2. Subjective Filtering of Outcomes

The Error: Deleting flawless outcomes like (P, P, P) from the sample space because the QA audit team is only searching for defects.

The Correction: Sample Space S represents objective physical reality and remains FIXED. Target events (E) shift depending on what question you ask, but pristine runs must stay in S.

3. Omitting the Baseline (Denominator Distortion)

The Impact on Classical Probability: P(E) = n(E) / n(S)

If n(S) is supposed to be 8, but you only listed 6 outcomes because you ignored flawless runs, your risk equations will report artificially inflated failure rates!

Industrial Application

3-Roll Textile Verification Scenario

You must inspect three consecutive rolls of fabric for weave defects. Each roll is categorized as either Flawless (F) or Defective (D).

Official Audit Verification Roadmap:

Master Sample Space:
S = {(F,F,F), (F,F,D), (F,D,F), (F,D,D), (D,F,F), (D,F,D), (D,D,F), (D,D,D)}
Cardinality: n(S) = 8

Event A ("At least 2 defective rolls"):
A = {(F,D,D), (D,F,D), (D,D,F), (D,D,D)}, n(A) = 4 → Compound Event

Event B ("All flawless rolls"):
B = {(F,F,F)}, n(B) = 1 → Simple Event

Classical Defect Probability: P(A) = n(A) / n(S) = 4 / 8 = 0.50 (50%)
